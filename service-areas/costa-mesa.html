Plumbing that fits Costa Mesa’s homes

Costa Mesa’s Eastside and the neighborhoods near 17th Street and downtown hold a lot of 1940s–1960s homes, many remodeled and re-remodeled over the years into a patchwork of pipe materials. Close enough to the coast to catch marine air, these homes see faster corrosion on exposed fittings and water heaters than inland cities do.

The Westside and the tracts around Mesa Verde and College Park are dominated by 1950s–1970s slab construction — copper under the slab, aging drains — while pockets near South Coast Metro trend newer and denser with condos and townhomes. Mesa Verde’s established streets bring mature trees and the clay-lateral root issues that come with them.

That range keeps our Costa Mesa schedule varied: an Eastside repipe, a Mesa Verde slab-leak locate, a water-heater swap near 17th Street. Whatever the job, we price it flat and in writing before starting.

What we fix most in Costa Mesa

Common Costa Mesa calls are slab leaks in the Westside and Mesa Verde tracts, salt-influenced corrosion on Eastside fittings and water heaters, and root-intruded sewer laterals under the older neighborhoods’ trees. We locate and camera before recommending anything.

We also untangle a lot of mixed-material plumbing from decades of remodels, plus steady water-heater replacement citywide. When we repair near the coast, we choose materials that stand up to the air.
